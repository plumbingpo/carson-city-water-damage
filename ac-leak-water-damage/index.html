AC Leak Water Damage in Carson City: What to Know

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Length of the leak, counted from the first drip to the day the system was stopped.
  • Whether the water is still clean or has to be handled as Category 2.
  • Where the unit sits: attic, closet or garage.
  • Rot in the stand beneath the air handler, which can mean rebuilding it.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • Loose or separated fittings on the condensate pipe that drip where nobody can see.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Nearby Flooring

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

Insulation Around the Unit

Insulation around an attic unit soaks up overflow and holds it against the ceiling. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Mold Near the Return

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

What We Do

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

1

Measure the Spread

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Dry the Structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. After that, our crew puts back the drywall, insulation, baseboard and floor and paints the repair to blend in.
